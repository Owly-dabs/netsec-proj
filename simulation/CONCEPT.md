# Plant model

A connected six-stage plant fills the viewport. Its initial orthographic view, neutral paper tones, four-tone shading, thin edges, tank bands, lid bolts, skids, flanges, collars, cabinets, racks, and pump mounts follow the anatomy style. Rotate, pan, or zoom the complete model.

Only the plant and a full-width bottom log with scrollable history and a vertical fade are visible. Click components to toggle independent attacks. Active components use red indications and equipment-specific motion. Pressure-surge demonstrations can release a soft plume. Blue water has soft moving light bands; clear pipe sections contain flow-driven rotors. Detected attack instances use orange outlines and lamps during the 3.5-second simulated operator response. Body tints stay weak, and water stays blue. Resolved components flash green and return to normal. Each attack has its own ID, result, and repair timer. A repair cannot clear a missed attack on another component, including one in the same stage. Missed attacks are checked again after three seconds; only a later positive result for that instance can start its repair. Missed targets remain under attack. Equipment-specific variants change the indicators, motors, displays, pulse, and steam. The compact list gives short state, target, and result messages. Monitoring runs from startup. Rare healthy-stage suspicions are verified and dismissed independently. Detection remains simulated.

The model has tanks and their feet, lids, necks, flange bolts, level gauges and bands; pumps with mounts, motors, rotor blades and two ports; cabinet shells, doors, displays, switches and handles; membrane racks with posts, rails, housings, caps and manifolds; and 24 pipes with elbows, collars, terminal flanges and supports. Each pipe is routed between named ports. The route audit checks terminal contact and equipment clearance.

The data boundary follows the checked-in loader and alarm code. Attack targets are ground truth. Ordered time-series signals are detector input. Predicted stages are detector output. These are separate fields. The page does not claim that a stage alarm identifies an individual physical component.
